Finance + AI

Where quantitative risk management meets AI-assisted engineering — how I'm using agent-based AI tooling to rebuild the way risk gets visualised, not just calculated.

GitHub Copilot Agent-Based AI VS Code Commodity Derivatives Risk Visualisation

Why this section exists

AI tooling has changed how fast a domain expert can turn what's in their head into working software. I spent three years learning exactly how commodity risk desks think — what a forward curve is actually telling you, why a time spread matters more than an outright price, where basis risk hides between related contracts. The gap was never the knowledge. It was the engineering time to turn that knowledge into a tool traders and risk managers would actually use.

That gap is closing. Working inside VS Code with GitHub Copilot and agent-based AI assistants, I can move from "here's how a risk manager thinks about this problem" to a working prototype in a fraction of the time it used to take — without outsourcing the judgment calls that actually matter. This section tracks that work: what I'm building, how I'm building it, and what I'm learning about AI as a tool for financial engineering rather than a replacement for financial judgment.

What I'm building now

I'm leading the development of an AI-assisted commodity risk dashboard — a ground-up rethink of how our desks visualise risk across a diverse derivatives book spanning oil, EU power and natural gas, LME metals, and agricultural markets. It's early-stage and evolving fast, built using GitHub Copilot and agent-based AI development workflows inside VS Code.

Status: Early-stage · actively in development · I'm leading the design and build.

What the dashboard covers

Forward Curves

Interactive views of the full forward curve for each commodity — shape, shifts over time, and what's driving contango or backwardation on a given day.

Time Spreads

Calendar spread monitoring across contract months, surfacing roll risk and carry dynamics before they show up in P&L.

Cross-Curve Basis Spreads

Relative-value views across related curves — where two markets that usually move together start to diverge, and by how much.

Trade Baskets

Aggregated statistics and exposure views for baskets of related positions, so risk that's invisible trade-by-trade becomes visible at the basket level.

Also in progress: a deal structure evaluation framework

Alongside the dashboard, I'm designing a framework for evaluating potential deal structures — a systematic way to compare structuring alternatives against the risk and P&L profile they'd create, rather than assessing each one from scratch. The goal is the same as the dashboard: take something that currently depends on an experienced person doing manual analysis, and make it faster, more consistent, and easier for stakeholders at every level to understand.

How I'm building it

The workflow is agent-based AI development inside VS Code: GitHub Copilot and AI agents handle a large share of the implementation — scaffolding, boilerplate, iteration — while I own the parts that require actual domain judgment: what the tool needs to show, what "correct" looks like for a given risk metric, and how a trader or risk manager should be able to interact with it under pressure, not just in a demo.

That division of labour is the actual point. AI tooling doesn't remove the need for someone who understands the market — it removes the friction between understanding the market and having something to show for it. Three years of daily Bloomberg monitoring across a live commodity book is what tells me which view actually matters to a risk manager at 8am; the AI tooling is what lets me build that view the same week instead of filing a request with an engineering team.

The bigger goal

Risk dashboards at most desks look the way they've looked for a decade — dense tables, static reports, context split across five different tools. I want to change that: intuitive enough that a new analyst can read it in a minute, interactive enough that a trader can stress-test an idea live in a meeting, and robust enough that a Director or MD can rely on it in a risk review. That's the standard I'm building toward, and AI-assisted development is what makes hitting it — as one person, alongside a full-time risk role — realistic.
